## Short arXiv/Zenodo abstract

A system that improves the machinery it uses to acquire capabilities raises two distinct questions: whether the modification helps, and where it stops helping. We report six frozen experiments, M107–M112, from the public Mira Genesis program. A bounded software lineage first acquires an operator that expands its lower interpreter, then acquires a rule that changes its acquisition machinery, and then uses lineage-controlled trials to produce a second machinery modification. Restored unchanged into a materially different carrier, the acquired cascade transfers positively on tested rows inside the producer's reachable failure census. On a realizable row outside that census, a fresh predecessor succeeds on 6/6 demands while both descendants fail 0/6, even though their constructive reach grows strictly on every world. A third machinery generation acquires a diagnostic policy that spends one scarce probe only where the inherited feature vocabulary does not determine an answer, succeeding under a budget that never-probe and always-probe strategies fail. Finally, one blind sealed generator invocation removes direct project selection/authorship of world contents. Diagnosis reproduces 24/24 and every scientific transfer outcome reproduces, including the harm, while the transfer arm remains negative at 22/24 because one blind world violates an inherited fixed-point certificate. No generality gate advances. We claim bounded, auditable acquisition-machinery adaptation, census-conditional negative transfer, and self-directed diagnosis—not open-ended recursive self-improvement, training-data independence, independent reproduction, or AGI.

## Primary claim

**Bounded multi-generation acquisition-machinery adaptation with census-conditional causal transfer, measurable negative transfer, and a third-generation diagnostic policy.**

## Secondary claims

1. An acquired machinery improvement can increase constructive capacity while strictly reducing realized competence outside the producer's reachable failure census.
2. The M110 harmful-transfer boundary is derivable from the producer's own census and conservative adoption rule before the consumer result is observed.
3. A later machinery generation can acquire a diagnostic policy that preserves a scarce probe on determined demands and spends it on an underdetermined one.
4. The expressibility of that diagnostic policy is causally dependent on a non-monotone operator acquired by the preceding machinery generation.
5. The M110 harm and M111 diagnosis reproduce on worlds the project did not select, while a blind-world fixed-point discrepancy remains honestly negative under the inherited M110 rule.
