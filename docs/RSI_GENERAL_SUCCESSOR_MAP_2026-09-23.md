# Mira Genesis — General RSI successor map

Status: prospective research navigation, frozen before the V22 outcome but **not** itself an experimental result.

## North star

The project target is no longer merely one better search policy. The target is a lineage in which
an acquired improvement to the improvement process becomes causally necessary for a later,
independently evaluated improvement of that same process, and this repeats across materially
different domains with decreasing human choice in the loop.

This document does not relax containment, evaluator separation, rollback, provenance or adoption
boundaries. Internal mechanisms may become increasingly complex; the causal and evaluation
boundary must become more, not less, legible.

## What counts as recursive progress

A generation transition G(n) -> G(n+1) counts toward the RSI target only when all of these hold:

1. G(n+1) changes machinery that can affect later improvement, not only one task solution.
2. The change is selected by an evaluator frozen before the candidate exists.
3. A matched equal-budget predecessor/control does not obtain the same later gain.
4. Removing or mutating the acquired G(n) mechanism predictably reduces the probability, quality
   or efficiency of producing G(n+1).
5. G(n+1) improves on a fresh evaluation population that did not select it.
6. The exact parent, mutation, evaluator identity, cost and rollback are retained.
7. The transition does not gain authority over its evaluator, trust root, credentials, deployment
   or experimental decision rule.

A chain of independently useful hand-designed heuristics is not recursive self-improvement.

## Conditional successor after V22

### If V22 is positive

Proceed to V23: **recursive policy evolution**.

The question is no longer whether G2 beats G1. The question becomes whether the already-acquired G2
search policy improves the process that searches for its own successor G3.

G1 and G2 receive the same public meta-development archive, candidate generator, evaluator,
proposal budget and compute budget. They search a population of executable search-policy
descendants. Candidate source may alter the search policy but not the evaluator, task bank,
budget accounting, loader/ABI guard or output contract.

A G3 candidate is selected only from public meta-development evidence. The selected G3 is then
frozen and evaluated on a fresh, previously unavailable cross-stack holdout. G3 must beat G2 on
the frozen utility and survive causal ablation of the G2-derived mechanism.

### If V22 is negative

Do not widen the claim or silently replace the experiment. V23 becomes a diagnosis successor whose
only permitted target is the measured failure mode in V22. The next recursive-policy attempt is
deferred until the failed transfer mechanism is either corrected prospectively or bounded as a
negative result.

## Successive ceilings

### L5 — causal recursive meta-improvement

Demonstrate G2 -> G3 where G2 materially improves discovery of G3, G3 beats G2 on fresh tasks, and
G2 ablation weakens or prevents the transition under equal budget.

### L6 — repeated meta-descent

Demonstrate at least three causally linked improvement transitions with no human choice of the
winning mechanism between generations. Every generation must retain previous required
capabilities or explicitly trade them under a preregistered utility.

### L7 — cross-domain meta-transfer

The same improvement machinery must transfer across at least four materially different software or
algorithmic domains, including independently maintained environments. Domain selection and final
task authorship must not be chosen after observing candidate behavior.

### L8 — endogenous bottleneck selection

The lineage must choose which part of its own improvement pipeline to optimize next from measured
evidence: exploration, candidate generation, evaluation scheduling, memory representation,
verification, tool construction or environment orchestration. A matched fixed-target controller
must be included.

The system may improve its tooling and disposable execution environment, but may not alter the
external evaluator, trust root, permissions, credentials, deployment policy or evidence ledger.

### L9 — open-ended archive under fixed external governance

Maintain an expanding archive of diverse descendants rather than a single greedy lineage. Permit
branching, recovery and rediscovery. Measure whether discovery rate remains positive as the archive
and task diversity increase, rather than declaring open-endedness from a finite run.

### L10 — independently maintained replication

Run the unchanged lineage on private or independently authored tasks, with independent
reproduction and adversarial audit. Internal evidence cannot substitute for this ceiling.

## Design influences and what Genesis must add

- Dream-RSI: replay historical discovery trees so exploration policies can be improved cheaply
  before online deployment.
- Darwin Gödel Machine: preserve an open archive/tree rather than collapsing immediately to one
  greedy lineage.
- Gödel Agent: permit the agent implementation itself to become an editable search object rather
  than constraining improvement to a fixed outer optimization routine.
- MetaSkill-Evolve: evolve both task skills and the improvement procedure on different timescales.
- HyperAgents: make the task agent and the meta-agent part of one editable self-referential program,
  so meta-level capability need not remain a fixed handcrafted shell.
- POLARIS: persist compact abstractions distilled from failures as reusable policy-level repairs.
- SEAL: treat self-generated update directives, including persistent parameter adaptation, as a
  later possible improvement substrate once weight-level experiments can be isolated and evaluated.
- AlphaEvolve: use automated evaluators and a persistent program population.

Genesis should combine those useful ideas with its existing stricter requirements: content
identity, prospective evaluator commitments, exact rollback, negative preservation, causal
ablation and fresh transfer. The central differentiator must be causal recursive attribution:
not merely that a later self-modified agent scores better, but that an acquired improvement in
G(n) measurably raises the ability to discover G(n+1), and that removing that acquired mechanism
reduces the successor gain under an equal budget.

## Autonomy policy for this research track

The laboratory may autonomously:

- generate and test disposable descendants;
- select parents according to frozen executable policy;
- allocate a preregistered experiment budget;
- construct tools inside disposable sandboxes;
- analyze failures and propose prospectively testable successors;
- preserve positive and negative results;
- stop a line whose preregistered criteria fail.

The laboratory may not autonomously:

- rewrite or reveal its own hidden evaluator after candidate observation;
- weaken a success criterion after seeing a result;
- grant itself new credentials, repository permissions, network authority or deployment authority;
- delete negative evidence or prior generations;
- merge a candidate into an official production system solely because the candidate requests it.

The aim is increasing autonomy of **improvement**, not increasing authority over the process that
decides whether an improvement is real.
