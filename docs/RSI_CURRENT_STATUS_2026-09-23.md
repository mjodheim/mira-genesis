## Conditional V23

V23 is preregistered before V22's result so that the meaning of "recursive improvement" cannot be
chosen after seeing the outcome.

If V22 is positive, V23 asks for a causal transition stronger than `G3 > G2`:

```text
G2 acquired mechanism
        ↓
better meta-search for policy descendants
        ↓
G3 selected without human winner choice
        ↓
strictly better fresh-holdout process utility than G2
```

A matched G1-meta arm, G2-mechanism ablation and fixed no-meta baseline are required. If G1 finds the
same G3 at the same cost, or G2 ablation leaves the transition unchanged, the result cannot be called
recursive attribution.

If V22 is negative, widening is prohibited. The successor must target the measured V22 failure.

See `RSI_GENERAL_SUCCESSOR_MAP_2026-09-23.md`.

## Longer target

The internal RSI research target is a sequence of causally linked improvements to the improvement
process itself:

```text
G1 → G2 → G3 → G4 → ...
```

A generation counts only when the previous acquired mechanism measurably improves the ability to
produce the next generation under a prospectively frozen evaluation and equal-budget controls.

Later ceilings require repeated meta-descent, cross-domain transfer, endogenous bottleneck
selection, an expanding archive of descendants and eventually independent/private evaluation.

## Claim boundary

None of the following is currently established by the V21–V23 line:

- open-ended or unrestricted recursive self-improvement;
- a closed generality gate;
- a general-agent or AGI claim;
- autonomous authority over evaluator, credentials, repository permissions, deployment or trust root.

Increasing autonomy of the **improvement process** is the research target. Authority over the
external process that decides whether an improvement is real remains separate.
