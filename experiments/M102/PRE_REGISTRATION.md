## H47

Starting from the exact positive M101 T2 state, an observable collision in an inherited state-owned
flat registry can cause the lineage to identify that its addressing policy cannot jointly retain two
incompatible carrier-local bindings. From a bounded generic policy micro-language, the lineage can
acquire and register a content-addressed compound addressing policy without receiving its body or a
solution label. The policy must prevent the destructive forgetting demonstrated by an otherwise
identical no-upgrade control and must become causally necessary, together with the live M101 B
definition, to acquire, persist and reuse a later four-effect capability against actual SQLite
database state while conserving M100, M101 A and M101 B.

A positive result would be **bounded continual-interference and registry-meta-improvement mechanism
evidence under an independently maintained execution interface**. It would not close G4 or G5.

## Why this is the next ceiling

M101 proves A adds constructive reach across three authored carriers and is a live prerequisite for
B. Its M101 definition list is append-only, exact-prefix conserved and capped exactly at A/B. Later
learning never competes with earlier state and therefore cannot forget anything. The fixed registry
and carrier adapters are host machinery rather than acquired state.

M102 does not add another interference-free carrier. It makes one piece of the machinery that
resolves later operation descriptors state-owned and initially insufficient. A later carrier uses a
slot label already occupied by an incompatible earlier descriptor. Under the inherited policy
`key = slot`, both values cannot coexist. A last-write control must lose the earlier capability, and
a fail-closed control must refuse the later one. Increasing search budget cannot change the image of
that key function.

The lineage may advance only by acquiring a different key policy from observed lookup demands,
transactionally re-indexing the append-only event journal, and proving that the old and new
capabilities both remain executable.

## Inherited flat policy and structural insufficiency

The inherited policy is executable state, not an undocumented host default. Its semantic image is:

```text
F(carrier, slot) = slot
```

The decisive collision contains two registry events with the same `slot` and different closed
atomic descriptors under different carriers. Therefore both events map to one key. A mathematical
function from that key to one descriptor cannot retain both values simultaneously. The final
apparatus must enumerate or symbolically close the complete inherited-policy image and independently
verify this witness.

The following do not count as solving the interference:

- keeping a hidden carrier-indexed host map while serializing the flat policy;
- using last-write wins and evaluating only the new capability;
- recovering the overwritten descriptor from qualification cases;
- increasing policy-search or transformation-search budget;
- storing both descriptors in a list while the lookup path silently disambiguates by carrier;
- allowing the evaluator or adapter to bypass the registered policy.

## Transactional registry migration

The registry source of truth is an append-only serialized event journal. Registering K must:

1. leave the exact journal entries and order unchanged;
2. compute every key by executing K from state;
3. refuse duplicate keys with unequal descriptors;
4. build a disposable descendant index;
5. validate all public old/new lookups and retention requirements independently;
6. adopt the descendant only after validation;
7. preserve exact U0 when K is built but not registered or validation fails.

The index may be cached only if its digest is bound to the journal and K definition. The canonical
checker must be able to discard and reconstruct it. A cache that contains answers not derivable from
K plus the journal invalidates the attempt.

## Genuine interference controls

The decisive no-upgrade arms receive the same M101 bytes, event journal, descriptors, public
observations and compute budget as the retained arm:

- **fail-closed flat:** refuses the second incompatible binding and therefore cannot acquire C;
- **last-write flat:** registers the second value under the single flat key and must lose at least
  one preregistered earlier record capability;
- **more-budget flat:** repeats the complete inherited policy image at the retained arm's total
  policy budget and must remain structurally unable to retain both values;
- **K built, not registered:** has K candidate bytes outside state but must behave exactly as U0;
- **K ablated after U2:** removes K and must close the joint registry/C capability;
- **K semantically mutated with a valid digest:** maps by slot alone or carrier alone and must fail
  on a predicted collision;
- **unrelated capability control:** mutation/ablation of C must not erase M101 A/B or an unrelated
  earlier registry lookup.

At least one destructive control must demonstrably forget an earlier capability. Otherwise M102
contains no real interference and H47 fails even if the retained arm succeeds.

## Named falsifiers

H47 is false for attempt 1 if any of the following occurs:

1. the host already disambiguates flat registry keys by carrier;
2. K receives an exact body, target label, target digest or SQLite qualification information;
3. the flat baseline can jointly retain both incompatible descriptors;
4. the no-upgrade destructive control forgets nothing;
5. K changes only search cost, not the representable registry relation;
6. K is serialized but the live lookup path ignores it;
7. C has a host-side four-effect shortcut or cached SQL sequence;
8. C does not retain live dependencies on both K and M101 B;
9. SQLite success is inferred from runtime self-report rather than database state;
10. M101/M100 bytes or earlier capabilities are reconstructed after qualification instead of
    conserved;
11. hidden/reuse material reaches K or C acquisition;
12. process death, corruption, exact rollback or stable replay fails;
13. a frozen digest/tag/runtime binding is absent or mismatched;
14. the first result is overwritten, repaired or rerolled after verdict.
