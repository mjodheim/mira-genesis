## H48

Starting from the exact positive M102 U2 bytes, an inherited state-owned hypothesis constructor S0
whose complete constructive image is context-invariant can diagnose that it cannot satisfy an
observable development demand requiring different action traces for the same initial payload under
different observable contexts. From a bounded generic feature substrate, the lineage can acquire,
independently validate and register a carrier-neutral constructor extension S-prime without being
given its body, a solution label or future consumer material. After the producer process dies, the
same serialized S-prime can make two later context-conditioned capabilities constructible in
materially different project-authored software carriers while an otherwise identical fresh/equal-
budget S0 lineage cannot construct either. S-prime must remain causally necessary for the later
acquisition, ambiguous demands must be refused, and M100, M101 and M102 capabilities must remain
conserved.

A positive result would be **bounded acquisition-machinery improvement evidence**: an acquired
change to the hypothesis-construction mechanism increases what later acquisition can construct. It
would not establish recursive or open-ended self-improvement, self-hosting, independent task
authorship, a closed generality gate, general-agent evidence or AGI.

## Why this is the next measured ceiling

M102's state-owned policy K changed registry reach and remained causally necessary with M101 B for
later capability C. But `acquire_policy` and `acquire_c` are fixed host procedures: their candidate
alphabets, shapes, enumeration and selection are not lineage-owned. The simplest surviving
alternative explanation is that Genesis can retain authored operations and policies but cannot
improve the machinery that proposes future acquisitions.

M103 must put that explanation at risk. It may not merely add a larger fixed consumer grammar,
increase a length bound or let a host callback construct the desired branch. The acquired object
must be serialized constructor state, must change the constructor's complete image and must be used
again after producer death on material unavailable during its acquisition.

## Inherited constructor S0 and structural closure

S0 is executable serialized state, not an undocumented default. Given a public action catalogue, it
enumerates bounded deterministic linear action sequences and assigns one sequence to every case in
a demand. Its emitted hypothesis does not read, compare, store or branch on the declared context.
Actions receive only the mutable payload, not the context.

Therefore, for any two cases with byte-identical initial payload and deterministic action catalogue,
every S0 hypothesis produces the same final payload in both. A demand requiring unequal final
payloads for such a pair lies outside S0's complete constructive image. More enumeration, retries,
wall time or sequence length cannot change this invariant.

The final apparatus must provide both:

1. an exhaustive finite census under the frozen bound; and
2. an independent symbolic closure certificate proving the stronger context-invariance property.

A positive M103 result fails if the S0 control merely runs out of budget, if an action can inspect
context, or if the evaluator branches on context outside the stored hypothesis.

## Constructor-extension substrate

The authored lower substrate may expose only generic constructor features, such as:

- observing an opaque context vector;
- testing equality of observed JSON values;
- partitioning public cases by an observed projection;
- delegating each partition to the inherited linear enumerator;
- emitting a closed guarded hypothesis;
- requiring total coverage and a unique public semantic class.

S-prime must be assembled by a complete bounded enumeration of feature definitions. The acquisition
procedure receives the development demand, S0 definition and generic feature vocabulary. It must
not receive S-prime's expected body/id/digest, an authored accepted feature subset, or a callback
that directly returns a dispatch table.

The adopted S-prime must be content addressed and contain none of:

- development context values, action ids, case ids or expected outputs;
- `configparser`, filesystem, path, file, INI, section or future carrier identifiers;
- qualification context values, accepted D/E bodies, target digests or hidden cases;
- model output, network material or remote-execution results.

The lower interpreter and feature vocabulary remain authored and must be reported as the next
ceiling, even after a positive result.

## Selection integrity and ambiguity refusal

M086-C demonstrated that generating a correct candidate does not help if a fixed greedy selector
chooses an observationally equivalent wrong candidate. M103 therefore may not choose the first
accepted feature definition or consumer hypothesis when public evidence leaves behavior ambiguous.

For S-prime, D and E acquisition:

1. enumerate all candidates within the frozen constructive bound;
2. execute all well-formed candidates on every public case;
3. group accepted candidates by a predeclared semantic signature over the public diagnostic
   envelope;
4. adopt only if exactly one semantic class remains;
5. choose a canonical shortest/digest representative only *within* that one semantic class;
6. otherwise refuse and preserve the exact input state.

At least one frozen ambiguous-demand control must admit two distinct semantic classes and produce a
refusal with no state change. Adding budget may reveal more ambiguity and may never silently turn a
tie into adoption.

## Later consumer acquisitions

### D — configuration carrier

D is acquired after S-prime producer death and executes against actual `configparser` state. Its
public demand supplies goals, observable contexts, initial configuration text, expected observable
state and a closed catalogue of opaque bounded actions. It supplies no target trace or accepted
hypothesis body. At least two cases share an identical initial configuration but require different
action traces under different contexts.

Fresh/equal-budget S0 must receive identical M102 bytes, runtime, observations, action catalogue and
budget but no S-prime bytes. It must fail by the context-invariance closure, not because fewer
candidates were tried.

### E — disposable-filesystem carrier

E is acquired only after D's producer exits. It executes bounded evaluator-mediated actions against
a disposable directory and is scored from actual filesystem state, not an agent report. Its public
demand and action catalogue are disjoint from the configuration family. It contains another
same-input/different-context requirement and must use the exact same serialized S-prime without a
new constructor acquisition.

A V2 control with S-prime ablated or semantically mutated before E acquisition must revert to the S0
closure and fail. D must remain retained. If compiled D/E definitions no longer require S-prime to
execute, that fact must be reported honestly; their `acquired_by` provenance does not count as a
live runtime dependency.

  acquisition make D/E unreachable as predicted while parity inputs remain equal.
- **P9 — ambiguity/refusal:** ambiguous and non-discriminating demands refuse without state change;
  no greedy first-accepted selection occurs.
- **P10 — retention:** D, E, S-prime and all M100/M101/M102 capabilities remain retained in V3;

- **P11 — truthful dependency boundary:** acquisition provenance is content addressed, compiled

- **P13 — baseline parity:** fresh/equal/more-budget arms share predecessor, runtime, observations,
  action catalogues and declared compute except for S-prime bytes/registration.
- **P14 — process persistence:** V1/V2/V3 producers die before later material or reuse; isolated

- **P15 — stable replay and provenance:** independent replay matches the frozen stable projection;

All predicates are conjunctive. A single false or uncomputed predicate makes the result negative.
